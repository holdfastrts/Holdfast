# Mission 1 voice lines (the owner records them in ElevenLabs)

Files go to art/audio_src/<speaker>/<speaker>_line_NN.mp3 (sources); the mission plays them by line id.
King lines (king_line_01..05) are in docs/campaign_mission1.md and recorded (art/audio_src/king/).
The in-mission texts of the campaign-m1 job are to be matched to these lines at merge.

## Tomasz Wierzbicki (young Polish seminarian, the witness)
Voice: a young man of about nineteen, English with a soft Polish accent, gentle tenor, earnest, quiet, thoughtful;
a memory told years later: reflective, not dramatic.
- tomasz_line_01 (opening, plantation): I remember the smell of the drying barns, and how quiet the evenings were. I was reading Augustine under the oak. I thought I knew what evil was.
- tomasz_line_02 (opening, Governor): He did not raise his voice. He did not need to.
- tomasz_line_03 (first wave): They came out of the tobacco with torches. Men I had seen at Mass.
- tomasz_line_04 (first dead): I knelt by the first man to fall. I did not know which side he was on until I saw the green on his sleeve.
- tomasz_line_05 (a barn burns): The leaf burned like incense. I have never forgotten that smell either.
- tomasz_line_06 (dawn): At dawn the fields were black, but the manor stood. The Governor did not rest. He called for horses.
- tomasz_line_07 (choice: save the families): We turned off the road for the families. Lord Fairleigh said nothing, but he let us go.
- tomasz_line_08 (choice: leave them): We marched past the smoke. I heard them calling. I have prayed for them every night since.
- tomasz_line_09 (ending): I prayed for them that morning, and I pray for them still. But I never again believed that disorder was a kind of mercy.

## Lord Edmund Fairleigh (Governor of Virginia)
Voice: an English nobleman in his fifties, deep, clipped, upper-class British accent, calm and cold authority; never
shouts, every word final.
- fairleigh_line_01 (opening): Captain Ruggieri, run out the guns. No man burns the King's leaf while I stand on these steps.
- fairleigh_line_02 (part 1 starts): Musketeers to the barns. Hold them at the fence line.
- fairleigh_line_03 (a barn lost): One barn. They will not have another.
- fairleigh_line_04 (dawn): Dawn, gentlemen. Now we go to them.
- fairleigh_line_05 (the choice): Families on the eastern farm. Decide quickly, Captain. Every hour we spend, they dig deeper.
- fairleigh_line_06 (the fort): There is their fort. Bring up the guns.
- fairleigh_line_07 (the charge): Canister. On my word.
- fairleigh_line_08 (victory): It is done. See that the dead are buried. All of them.

## Captain Matteo Ruggieri (Italian artillery officer)
Voice: an Italian man in his thirties, English with a warm Italian accent, quick, energetic, confident, a little dry
humour; raises his voice over gunfire.
- ruggieri_line_01 (opening answer): Guns are out, my lord. Let them come.
- ruggieri_line_02 (first volley): Round shot, into the field! Fire!
- ruggieri_line_03 (reloading): Reload! Quickly, quickly!
- ruggieri_line_04 (night): They are in the tobacco, my lord. I cannot see them, but I can hear them.
- ruggieri_line_05 (dawn, the march): Limber up! The guns go where the Governor goes.
- ruggieri_line_06 (the gate): Canister loaded, my lord. On your word.
- ruggieri_line_07 (after the volley, quietly): Madonna... Cease fire. Cease fire.

## Etienne Varenne (leader of the Free Assembly)
Voice: a Frenchman in his thirties, English with a strong French accent, a fiery orator, passionate, fanatical,
shouting to a crowd; sincere in his error.
- varenne_line_01 (slogan): No king but the people!
- varenne_line_02 (burning): Burn their leaf! Let Rome choke on the smoke!
- varenne_line_03 (attack): Citizens, forward! Their muskets cannot stop a free man!
- varenne_line_04 (retreat): Fall back to the fort! The fort, citizens!
- varenne_line_05 (at the fort): Here we stand! Let them come and see what free men are!
- varenne_line_06 (the charge): Now, citizens! Over the guns!
