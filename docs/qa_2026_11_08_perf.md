# QA study: performance (0.33.8, branch qa-perf)

Part of the owner's "overall study and complete systematic test of the game" (2026-11-07). The question: where does the
game spend its time and memory, on every map, in big battles, on screen, over a long session, in the saves and replays,
and in the web download; what got faster here; what is left and what to expect on a mid-range phone.

## The short answer

- **The late-game slowdown is the units' own work, growing with the number of units.** Over a computer game to 15:00 on
  every 1v1 map, one game tick costs about 1.5 ms at 1:00 and 11 to 14 ms on average at 10:00 to 15:00 on this machine
  (150 to 230 units; 24 ms on the busiest map). Two thirds to three quarters of it is the units' tick (moving, shoving, looking for targets, animating); the
  computers' thinking, the buildings and the rest of the game share the remainder. Nothing else grows with time: no leak,
  no part of the game that gets slower by itself. It is the army sizes.
- **Team maps are much heavier**: 300 to 600 units by 10:00, 25 to 50 ms a tick here, 33 to 76 frames a second with 1%
  lows of 7 to 15 (section 2).
- **On a mid-range phone** expect a late 1v1 game tick to take 15 to 30 ms (30 ticks a second: half to most of each
  second goes to the game itself), so 25 to 40 frames a second in the late game and 15 to 25 in a big fight; a late 2v2
  or bigger is not smooth on a phone. Section 7 explains the estimate.
- **What was fixed here** (no rule changes; the game plays out exactly the same, checked unit by unit in 12 big battles
  and 3 whole computer games):
  1. The **autosave's freezes**: writing the save and the match log every 15 s froze the game for 70 to 680 ms whenever
     the storage was busy (14 times in this study's games). The files are now written on a worker thread; the save's
     text is still made on the game's thread, in steps, and its first step is lighter.
  2. **Memory**: the Brood Mother's outgrown stages (about 70 MB of pictures each) are let go; every match's pictures are
     let go at the menu (a session kept every picture it had ever shown: 100 to 260 MB per matchup). A Brood mirror's
     pictures at 9:43: 261 MB -> 121 MB.
  3. The **silhouettes** of units hidden behind buildings and big creatures (a part nobody had timed): a third less
  work, up to 16 ms in one frame in a big musket battle with cannons.
  4. The **first picture of a kind about to appear** (an egg's hatchling, a unit in training, a Horseman's dismounted
     self, the Mother's first egg) is now loaded ahead in the background: those first shows cost 100 to 400 ms in one
     frame in this study's games (a Spitter, an egg, a Tyrannosaur, a dismounted Horseman, a Ballista, a Springer).
  5. **The web version**: the campaign's paintings and voices were in the code download that every update downloads
     again: an update now downloads 4.4 MB instead of 13.7 MB.
- **The biggest single freezes left** are the computers' long searches (the Clockwork computer's next Clocktower site,
  at about 1:45 in nearly every game with a Clockwork computer: 130 to 460 ms on 1v1 and 2v2 maps, 1,040 ms on The
  Sundered Realm; the Crown's walled natural plan; the Brood's morph and father picks: 85 to 370 ms), a whole side's army dying at once when a
  player is out (200 to 930 ms) and the first frame of the HUD (about 100 ms, once). Section 6 lists them with a
  suggested fix each; they would change WHEN the computer decides something, so they are the owner's call.

## How it was measured

- The machine: a cloud computer with 2 cores, no graphics card. Godot 4.7, headless (`--fixed-fps 60`: every frame
  moves the game 1/60 s whatever it costs, so the times are what each frame really costs; 30 game ticks a second).
- Most runs shared the machine with another run (two cores, two runs), and one machine restart lost a few runs that were
  started again. Single frames are noisy: a frame can wait for the other run. The averages and the counts (units, draw
  calls, memory) are solid; a lone slow frame with no part named ("the rest") is most likely the machine.
- New and extended tools (all in this branch):
  - `tools/perf_bench.gd`: now also times the **game tick itself** (the physics step: units, buildings, projectiles,
    the game; mean / 95th / max per minute), the program's **memory** (resident, static, resources, objects, nodes), the
    replay's size, and finer parts of the game's tick (`game/econ`, `game/gears`, `game/zones`, `game/swamp`,
    `game/end+ghosts`, `game/stats`, `silhouettes`, `autosave/begin|step|final|log`). `savestate:FILE` writes the match
    as a save at the end (for the drawing runs).
  - `tools/qa/perf_matrix.sh`: a Hard computer game on every map (27), factions rotated so every pairing and mirror
    appears; minute tables in `docs/perf/qa/before/<map>.csv` (+ .md), `tools/qa/perf_report.py` makes the tables below.
  - `tools/stress_swarm.gd` scenario `pair a:<faction> b:<faction>` and `tools/qa/battle_matrix.sh`: every pairing at
    100 and 200 a side (Crown: Musketeers + a tenth Field Cannons; Brood: 3/4 Raptors + 1/4 Spitters; Clockwork: 3/4
    Clock Soldiers + Springers + a tenth Winders), with a trace of the whole state every game second.
  - `tools/qa/render_bench.gd`: the drawing's cost from a late-game save, four views (the army, the base, zoomed out,
    a fogged corner), at 1920x1080 and a phone-size window, as a player and as a spectator with the panels open.
  - `tools/qa/mem_session.gd`: several matches one after another in one program (as a phone keeps the game open),
    memory and the picture cache after each.
  - `tools/qa/save_cost.gd`: the autosave's and the MIXED replay keyframe's parts timed on a late-game state.
- The data: `docs/perf/qa/before/` (every map's minute table), `docs/perf/qa/battles/` (each battle before and after,
  and the two drawn battles), `docs/perf/qa/render/` (the drawing runs).
- "Same game" checks: the battle traces (12 battles, 2,700 to 9,900 lines each) and `perf_bench.gd trace:` of three
  9-minute computer games (Greenhold Crown v Brood, Highmoor Crown v Clockwork, Twin Fords Brood v Brood), before and
  after: identical. `tools/test_save_job.gd`: PASS. `tools/test_brood_controls.gd` (3 failures) and `tools/test_replay.gd`
  (speed_4x, a timing check) fail the same way on 0.33.7 without these changes: left to the qa-tests job.

## 5. Memory, saves and replays

### Memory over a game and over a session

- In a game: the resident memory (headless, so pictures count as memory) grows by 40 to 110 MB over a game without the
  Brood and by 180 to 250 MB with the Brood: the pictures of the kinds that appear (loaded ahead so they don't freeze).
  Objects, nodes and bodies come and go with the armies; the match log (230 to 780 lines) and the replay (2 to 6 MB on
  disk by 15:00) are small. No leak in a game.
- The Brood Mother's three stages are 70 + 70 + 64 MB of pictures (uncompressed in the graphics memory, which a phone
  shares with everything else), and all three stayed loaded once she had grown. NOW: a stage no Mother on the field has
  shown for 30 s is let go (scripts/art_warm.gd, `_release`). Twin Fords Brood v Brood at 9:43: the picture cache 261 MB
  -> 121 MB, the program's static memory 671 -> 568 MB, the game the same to the end.
- Over a session (6 matches in a row, `mem_session.gd`): the picture cache kept every picture ever shown (433 pictures,
  117 MB after one Crown v Brood; 647, 133 MB after three matchups of 4 minutes; far more in long games). NOW it is
  emptied when you go back to the menu (main.gd `_to_menu`); the next match loads its own while it starts (ArtWarm).
  Other than that a session grows by about 5 to 7 MB a match (objects left in caches: maps of steps, memos): no real leak.

### The autosave (every 15 s)

On a late-game state (Greenhold 11:00, 211 units; `save_cost.gd`, five times each):

| part | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| the state taken (capture), one frame | 3 to 12 ms | the same (it must be one frame: the state of one moment) |
| the head's text (with the match log) + copies, the same frame | 3 to 12 ms | 2.6 to 4 ms (a copy now; its text is made on the next step) |
| the units' text, in steps of 100 units on frames without a game tick | 2 to 6 ms each | the same |
| writing the save (156 kB) and the match log | 1 to 6 ms, **70 to 680 ms when the storage was busy** (14 times in this study's games: autosave/final and autosave/log in the spike lines) | on a worker thread: nothing on the game's frame |

The writes are on a worker thread (SaveGame.write_async; anything that reads, writes or removes the save or the log
waits for it first: leaving, the end of a match, CONTINUE, a sync save when the app goes to the background). The web
build has no threads and writes as before.

### Replays

- FULL (computers' default): its pictures (`replay/record`) are 0.25 to 0.4 ms a frame late in a game, on frames without
  a game tick; 2 to 6 MB by 15:00.
- MIXED (the phones' default): a keyframe every 10 s = the same capture as the autosave (3 to 13 ms) + its bytes (2.4 ms,
  248 kB, 22 kB after zstd): one 6 to 15 ms frame every 10 s here. Left as it is (section 6).

## 6. What is left, with the measured cost and a suggested fix

The single freezes (each once in a while; from the spike lines of this study's games):

## 7. What to expect on a mid-range phone

- This machine's core runs the game's scripts at about the speed of a 2021-2023 mid-range phone's big core (Cortex-A76
  to A78 class); a phone also slows down by 20 to 40% when it warms up in a long game. So take this study's numbers and
  multiply them by about 1.2 to 2.
- 1v1, early game (under 80 units): the tick is 1 to 5 ms here, 2 to 10 on a phone: smooth.
- 1v1 at 10:00 to 15:00 (150 to 230 units): 12 to 17 ms here, about 15 to 35 ms on a phone. At 30 ticks a second that
  is 45% to all of each second for the game alone before anything is drawn: 25 to 40 frames a second, lower in fights,
  and the Settings' MIXED replay keyframe and the autosave each add one longer frame (now without the storage freezes).
  That is the slowdown the owner saw growing late in the game.
- A big battle (200 a side) on screen: 13 to 15 ms a tick here for the Crown, plus 1,300+ draw calls: 10 to 20 frames a
  second on a phone during the clash.
- 2v2 and bigger late: not smooth on a phone (25 to 50 ms a tick here, so 30 to 100 ms on a phone: the game slows down
  below real time on the 3v3 and 4v4 maps).
- Memory: a Brood game's pictures are now about 120 MB instead of 260 MB late, and a new match no longer keeps the last
  match's; the phone's graphics memory is the first thing to run out on a 4 GB phone, so this matters more than the
  numbers suggest.

## Claude's choices for the owner

- "Every map, all three factions": one game per map with the factions rotated (every 1v1 pairing and mirror appears on
  three maps or more; team maps mix all three), not three games per map: 27 games of up to 15 minutes already took about
  six hours of the machine.
- The 2v2 maps after Twin Crowns ran to 12:00 and the 3v3 / 4v4 maps to 10:00 (not 15:00): a 2v2 to 15:00 took 35
  minutes of the machine, and the trend is already clear by then.
- Before / after timings of whole games are not compared minute by minute: the fixes don't touch the tick, and on a
  shared 2-core machine the run-to-run noise (10 to 15%) is bigger than any change. The fixes are measured where they act
  (the silhouettes' part, the save's parts, memory) and the games were checked to be the same games.
- Nothing that changes when the computer decides something, or how units behave, was changed (the freezes in section 6
  are listed, not fixed).
