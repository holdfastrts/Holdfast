# The campaign's structure (owner's decisions, 2026-11-11)

Built on the earlier decisions in CLAUDE.md (one story spine, flashbacks, the King narrates truthfully, the Crown and
the Church are the good guys, two storylines crossing three times, year about 2767, mission 1 = Virginia).
Claude's full options list was given in chat on 2026-11-11; these are the owner's answers.

## Shape: all three together

- A: ACTS IN ORDER OF TIME (Prologue, Act 1 Revolutions, Act 2 the Breaking, Act 3 the Fall of the Colonies, the Finale
  in the present).
- B: BRAIDED STORYLINES inside each act (the Crown spine, the Clockwork line, the Brood line), meeting at the crossings.
- C: a GLOBAL WAR MAP to pick missions from inside an act: territories held and lost; choices change it for real.
  It grows into a three-way map in Act 3 (the Clockwork and the Brood fight each other as well as the Crown).

## The frame

- The King narrates; listeners come and go as needed (the heir, old Tomasz, generals, advisers).
- The story catches up with the present; the finale is played in the present.
- The heir is seen learning in the frame and then PUTS IT IN PRACTICE: heir missions/scenes that echo a lesson
  just told (form and number open).

## The three crossings (decided)

1. A CHURCH COUNCIL IN ROME judges the new machines and the new creatures; the priest and the explorer testify.
2. BRAZIL caught between a Brood swarm on one border and a Clockwork city on the other: the Crown chooses which to
   fight first (a diamond branch with consequences).
3. THE GLOBAL WAR BEGINS: the Clockwork and the Brood both turn on the Crown, some rebels join one side or the other,
   and the Clockwork and the Brood attack each other too (three-way on the global map).

## Storylines

As proposed: the Crown spine (Tomasz from seminarian to missionary to old cardinal); the Clockwork line (the clockmaker,
proud and wrong; the priest, right); the Brood line (the Catholic explorer; the scientists, deceived about what they
control; the Brood as nature unleashed by pride). The Free Assembly's ideas live on in the guild and the scientists.

## Both sides: all three ways, by campaign mode

- SOLO: pick a side where a battle is mirrored.
- CO-OP: two players on the same side.
- CO-OP VERSUS: each player on his own faction's side of the same campaign (needs a hub per faction).
- Every mission is written so it CAN be mirrored or one-sided; the canon timeline is always the Crown's account.

## Choices and consequences

Forces, heroes, places and story changes all carry forward, and they show on the global map (who holds what).
A lost battle returns as a reclaim mission. About one real choice per three missions.
ACT 1's CHOICE: spare or execute the rebel leader (Etienne Varenne), not "how hard to punish a rebel city".

## Endings

Two endings (Rome holds and the heir is crowned / Rome falls but the faith carries on), plus minor variations
compiled from the choices made (who is alive, which places stand, who stands with you).

## Hubs

THREE hubs, one per faction (needed for co-op versus). Today's hub is a placeholder drawn in code; options for real art
are in the chat of 2026-11-11 (painted scene + hotspots, layered painting with code-driven animation, walkable room,
the war map as the shared table).
DECIDED (owner, 2026-11-11 06:03): option 2 (a layered painting, motion in code: smoke, candles, breathing, gears) for
the three rooms, plus option 4, the global war map as each hub's table, but DIFFERENT for each faction (Claude's
proposal: the Crown a parchment map with pins and wax seals on the King's desk; the Clockwork a brass mechanical map
with turning rings; the Brood the scientists' specimen chart, later a map scratched into the nest floor).

## Missions

As much variety as possible: hold, march, escort, siege, rescue/capture, fog hunts, hero missions, choice missions,
versus and co-op missions. NO NAVAL in the engine: sea travel and sea battles only in the story (stills).

## Size

Medium to large: as many missions as a complete, good story takes. Built act by act.
